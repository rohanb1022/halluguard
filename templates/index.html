False Positives (3 cases)

Cause: Lexical polarity check triggered false alarms when a fact was phrased with negative qualifiers (e.g. "does not require recurrent connections").

2. Problem Statement

Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini produce fluent, authoritative text even when factual details are completely incorrect. This limitation arises from next-token autoregressive prediction without verification capabilities, knowledge cutoff boundaries, and finite context windows. Users (especially students) cannot differentiate between reliable and fabricated statements.

3. Proposed GenAI Solution

HalluGuard serves as an automated post-generation guardrail. It decomposes generated text into individual claims, matches each claim to trusted evidence via TF-IDF cosine similarity, executes deep feature checks (numbers with units, named entities, negation polarity, and semantic coverage), and produces an annotated color-coded answer alongside a guarded answer containing only verified facts with source citations.

5. Responsible AI Analysis (Mandatory Rubric)

Risk Category Present? Possible Impact Mitigation Strategy
Bias Yes (limited) Dependent on KB content; English-only rules may unfairly flag non-standard English phrasing. Transparent evidence citation, open multi-domain sources, and human audit review.
Hallucination Yes LLM outputs hallucinate; detector can have false alarms (FP) or missed contradictions (FN). Multi-tiered verification, guarded answer generation, and human-in-the-loop review.
Privacy No / Low No user text is transmitted to external clouds or third-party servers; runs 100% locally. Complete offline processing on localhost with no telemetry or external data logging.
Misuse / Security Yes (limited) Users might blindly trust a "Verified" badge without checking primary sources. Clear UI disclaimers that HalluGuard is a verification aid, not infallible mathematical proof.
